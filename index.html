Biography

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Civil and Environmental Engineering at the University of Virginia, advised by Negin Alemazkoor and co-advised by Majid Shafiee-Jood. My research lies at the intersection of scientific machine learning and physical simulation, with a focus on graph neural networks, neural operators, and geometry-informed deep learning.

I develop scalable and reliable surrogate models for complex physical systems, with applications in flood forecasting, inundation modeling, and stormwater hydraulics. My work explores how geometry, spatial connectivity, boundary conditions, and physical constraints can be incorporated into learning frameworks to improve predictive accuracy, generalization, and stability in long-horizon predictions.

My broader research interests include physics-informed machine learning, domain adaptation, multi-fidelity learning, and uncertainty-aware modeling.

Before joining UVA, I earned my M.Sc. from Tarbiat Modares University and my B.Sc. from the University of Kurdistan.

Research

Scientific Machine Learning

Geometry-informed graph neural networks and neural operators for scalable, stable, and transferable simulation of complex physical systems.

AI for Environmental Systems

Developing advanced AI methods for flood forecasting, inundation modeling, and stormwater management, with a focus on improving predictive accuracy, early warning capabilities, and water infrastructure resilience.
